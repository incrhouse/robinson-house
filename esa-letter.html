What is an ESA letter?

Your pet is not just a pet — and this letter proves it

An Emotional Support Animal (ESA) letter is a formal document written by a licensed mental health professional stating that you have a diagnosed mental health condition and that your animal provides therapeutic support that alleviates symptoms of that condition.

A legitimate ESA letter can:

  • Allow your ESA to live with you in housing that otherwise has a no-pets policy under the Fair Housing Act
  • Protect you from pet deposits or pet fees in many housing situations
  • Provide documentation for housing accommodation requests

⚠ Important: ESA letters are not the same as service animal certifications

ESA letters apply to housing accommodations under the Fair Housing Act. They do not grant access to public places, restaurants, or stores. Airlines are not required to accommodate ESAs under current federal rules. Dr. Cleve will explain exactly what your letter covers during your evaluation.

Why it matters who writes your letter

The internet is full of fake ESA letters. Here is the difference.

Many websites sell ESA letters for $30-$100 with no real evaluation — just a questionnaire and an automatic approval. These letters are not legitimate and landlords are increasingly rejecting them.

A valid ESA letter must come from a licensed mental health professional who has actually evaluated you and determined that an emotional support animal is clinically appropriate for your diagnosed condition. That is exactly what Dr. Cleve provides — a thorough clinical evaluation followed by a letter written on his professional letterhead, with his license number included.

Dr. Cleve does not write ESA letters for everyone who requests one. He only issues letters when he determines they are clinically justified. This is what makes his letters credible and defensible.
